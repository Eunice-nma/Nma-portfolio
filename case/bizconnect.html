The problem

The deadline was tight, and the app was still at an early stage, with basic login, a home screen that needed work, and hard-coded data. We had to prioritise the essential features, address development constraints, and establish a steady delivery pace to launch on time.

How it came together

Set up to move fast

Early on, I pushed for investing in a well-structured app foundation before adding more features. On a tight deadline it can feel like a detour, but it's what lets a team move fast: developers can build different features at the same time without blocking each other, and every new feature is quicker to add and easier to maintain. That early investment is a big part of how we launched in four months.

Turning ideas into a clear product scope

Stakeholder ideas came from different directions, so I translated them into defined requirements, prioritised features, and delivery plans. This helped the team focus on what the marketplace needed for its first release rather than trying to build everything at once.

Making multi-vendor shopping work

A key part of the marketplace experience was allowing customers to buy from multiple vendors in one order. I prioritised a multi-vendor cart and checkout flow, ensuring customers could complete purchases without having to transact separately with each seller.

Getting the MVP to market

With a lean development team, I coordinated priorities, requirements, and delivery across the project to keep development moving. We launched the iOS and Android apps on the App Store and Google Play in under four months, with payments, vendor listings, order processing, and payouts in place.

Results

  • Launched on the App Store and Google Play within 4 months.
  • After launch: media in chat, and business pages vendors can customise with layouts, fonts and colours.

What I learned

  • Isolate a problem in a tiny test screen. It found a stubborn push-notification crash fast, and I used it across the whole project.
