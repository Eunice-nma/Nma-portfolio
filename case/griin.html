The problem

My thoughts, moods, and goals were scattered across diaries, notes, and random to-do lists. I wanted one place to bring everything together without the clutter that often comes with all-in-one apps. So every design decision came down to one question, does this make my day easier, or just give me more to look at?

How it came together

A home screen that stays calm

The day's journal prompt doubles as the Home screen background, offering gentle inspiration to write without adding clutter or another card. Below it sit today's tasks, a reminder of your next goal, and your habits. There's also a simple mood and anxiety check-in, making it easy to see how you're feeling as part of your daily routine.

Feelings, checked in gently

Anxiety is a smooth slider instead of a number scale, with soft colours and words that describe how it might actually feel, not generic labels. Details like what's behind it, such as work, sleep, family or money, only appear once you start interacting. Over a week or a month, it all adds up so you can spot the patterns that shape your days.

Big goals, small steps

Set long-term goals and get gentle reminders to keep them in sight. You can break each goal into smaller tasks for today or the days ahead. Big goals don't happen overnight. It's the small steps you take consistently that bring you closer to them.

Privacy on your terms, and a little brain break

Privacy needs change, and the app puts you in control. You can choose to lock your journal every time you leave the screen or keep it unlocked throughout each app visit. If you ever need to secure it immediately, there's also a lock button right on the journal screen. And when you need a little break from writing or planning, a daily word game offers a fun way to exercise your brain and gives you another reason to return.

Small details I thought about

  • A journal that lasts: sync is optional, but if you turn it on you can come back years later, from a completely different device, and find every thought.
  • Entries are encrypted on your phone before they sync.
  • Journal prompts arrive like a message from a friend, for the days you don't know what to write.

What it taught me

Building Griin from scratch gave me a deeper understanding of what it takes to turn an idea into a working product. From defining the core experience and deciding what belonged in the first version to working through technical requirements and development, I learned to connect product decisions to real user needs and ensure every feature had a clear purpose.
