The problem

Customer support agents were already helping some users shop on AliExpress, all by hand. Users sent screenshots of their carts, agents placed the orders, and every order meant a lot of back-and-forth in chat to get the details right. Support wanted to make the service official, so they brought the product team a challenge: show us how it works today, and turn it into something easier for users and agents alike.

How it came together

Screenshot in, total out

Instead of agents collecting screenshots and details over chat, users upload screenshots of their AliExpress cart straight into the app. We used AI to read each item from the screenshot and add up the total, and if it misses something, users can ask it to try again.

Fees and balance, upfront

Before placing an order, users see every item, our fee and the grand total, and confirm it. We also check their balance, and if it isn't enough, we send them straight to fund their USD account so they can finish the order.

Shoppers take it from there

Confirmed orders, with delivery details, go to the shoppers on their own dashboard that only they can see. Each item comes as copyable text, so they don't have to type anything out. As they shop, they update the order's progress in the app, and users get notifications and emails instead of manual messages, right up until their order arrives.

Testing showed us the gaps

We had about a month, from getting the challenge in October to a November launch. But testing with users the agents already shopped for exposed a major gap: sold-out items, or promos we couldn't access, broke the flow, and we had to cancel most test orders. Rather than ship something that didn't work just to hit the date, we let shoppers flag an item as needing an extra amount or unavailable. Users then choose to go ahead with it, remove it, or cancel the whole order, and the shopper knows exactly what to do next.

Results

  • Launched in mid-November. We missed Black Friday, so we shifted our focus to Christmas and Valentine's shopping.
  • A couple of hundred orders in the first six months.
  • Support agents moved from long chats to placing orders officially, and earning a fee for it.

What I learned

  • Hitting a date matters, but not more than shipping something that works. A short delay saved us from a launch full of cancelled orders.
  • In fintech, trust is a feature. Clear totals, notifications and tracking emails made people comfortable placing orders.
